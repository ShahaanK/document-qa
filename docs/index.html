01 · Why this year

Nobody in this building needs another dashboard. The margin is leaking upstream of the dashboard.

Every pressure on this industry right now is a data problem wearing a logistics costume. You cannot re-route sourcing, re-price a program, or hold a promised ship date on a catalog that lives in eleven spreadsheets and a rep's memory.

02 · The spine

Five stages, one system. This is what “end-to-end” actually has to mean.

Point tools each solve a slice and then hand you an integration bill. We build the whole lifecycle on one product model, so a fabric grade change in stage 02 re-prices stage 03, re-forecasts stage 04, and still resolves a parts claim in stage 05.

01

Source

Tariff and landed-cost intelligence, per SKU, per origin, per scenario.

›HTS classification proposed for every line on your price list, with the reasoning shown

›Duty + ocean freight + drayage modeled to LDP from your real FOB terms

›“Move this frame program to Vietnam” — full re-cost across every variant in minutes

02

Catalog

The unglamorous one that makes the other four possible.

›A 4,000-line sheet becomes a clean variant matrix: frame finish × sling × COM/COL grade × cushion

›Dimensions, weights, cartons, freight class and care specs normalized — not retyped

›Imagery and 3D assets tagged to the exact configuration they depict, so nothing ships against the wrong photo

03

Sell

Configure, price and quote — standing up, on a showroom floor, in under a minute.

›A twelve-piece deep-seating group priced with the right fabric grade, program discount and freight rule

›Guided selling in plain language: “coastal, full sun, needs to seat nine” → three real, in-stock configurations

›Retailer-specific price lists and regional terms respected automatically — no rep math, no margin surprises

04

Fulfill

Promise dates that hold, and a pre-season buy you can defend.

›Lead times forecast from your actual vendor and container history, not a flat 12–16 weeks

›Pre-season buy recommendations by climate zone and store — Phoenix and Minneapolis are not the same season

›Container-level exposure: what's on the water, what it costs to hold, what slips if it's late

05

Service

The part of outdoor furniture that lasts twenty seasons — and generates tickets for all twenty.

›Customer sends a photo of a 2019 sling chair; the agent identifies the program and the replacement sling SKU

›Warranty claims triaged against the original configuration, not a guess

›Cushions, glass tops, umbrella canopies and hardware — the long tail that eats a service desk
